## A. Non-negotiable operating objective

The objective is not “make AI videos,” “start a content channel,” or “do outreach.” The objective is:

> **Maximize legitimate, attributable cash generation from a $0-upfront starting point, strongly preferring first revenue within roughly seven days, while maximizing automation, scalability, long-term upside, provider resilience, and the user’s freedom from manual prompting/work.**

The user does not want to keep supplying ideas. Codex must search for ideas, capabilities, free resources, signals, markets, monetization paths, and challengers itself.

The user expects the system to optimize globally rather than return the first plausible business idea. Maintain a champion strategy and continuously try to defeat it with better challengers. A challenger replaces the incumbent only when evidence says it is materially superior under the user’s actual constraints.

No one can guarantee first revenue inside seven days. Treat seven days as the optimization target, not a promise.

## B. Current champion and why it won

The current champion is **signal-driven B2B revenue capture**:

`economic/buying signal -> qualified target -> reachable decision-maker -> proof asset -> compliant outreach -> payment -> fulfillment -> attribution -> reinvestment`

The reasoning is that it currently dominates other $0-upfront paths on near-term cash probability, controllability, demonstrability, margin, and ability to automate.

Do NOT interpret this as a permanent niche or a generic creative agency. Media/AI creative is a weapon inside the system, not the business definition.

This ranking is a research conclusion, not an immutable truth. Re-score with fresh evidence when conditions change.

1. Signal-driven B2B service / revenue capture — current champion for first cash.
2. Qualified lead / appointment brokerage — extremely strong and should be integrated as a monetization rail.
3. Research / audit / competitive-intelligence products — strong for fast cash and automation.
4. Digital products + organic distribution — scalable but generally slower to first attributable cash than targeted B2B.

Other challenger classes discussed: micro-SaaS, niche newsletters, data products, sponsorship/licensing/stock-media, white-label agency fulfillment, performance-based services, local-business intelligence, software referrals, owned products, and other credible zero-capital opportunities discovered later.

### Adobe Express
Use for fast reusable commercial design work: templates, social assets, flyers, proposals, promo graphics, basic animation, and other design tasks that should not consume scarce Runway generation credits.

### Descript
Use for higher-quality editing, transcription, cleanup, repurposing, transcript-driven editing, clip extraction, project publishing, and AI-assisted editing. It is a video-production fallback/partner rather than a reason to spend more Runway credits.

### Runway
Use primarily for high-value generative image assets under the currently verified free-workspace limitations. Reserve credits for work with a direct monetization hypothesis or strong reusable-system value.

## M. Provider independence and graceful degradation

No revenue-critical capability should have one provider if a free fallback is feasible.

Examples:
- `video.render`: vidIQ/Descript -> client footage -> licensed/free B-roll -> stills + motion graphics -> local FFmpeg composition
- `voice`: cloud voice -> local TTS -> caption-only
- `prospecting`: Apollo -> Hunter -> public hiring/ad/business signals -> direct website research
- `publishing`: Metricool -> vidIQ/official APIs -> export/manual publish
- `analytics`: Windsor.ai -> Metricool -> platform analytics -> UTM/click/payment attribution
- `CRM/state`: HubSpot when connected -> protected private database/sheet/local store; never commit private operational records into the public repo

The point is resilience, not accumulating tools for its own sake.
