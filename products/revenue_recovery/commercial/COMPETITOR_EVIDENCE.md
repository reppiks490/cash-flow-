## Wedge to test before building a general-purpose clone

Instead of replacing existing CRMs, offer an **operator-supervised leakage audit and gap-closure service**:

1. With the owner's authority, inspect a documented process map and anonymized/synthetic examples. Never ingest real records without production privacy/QA gates.
2. Show where follow-up is missing, disabled, mistimed or impossible across the operator's actual workflow, with evidence and a human-review queue.
3. Recommend configuring already-paid-for Jobber/Housecall Pro/ServiceTitan features first when adequate, rather than selling duplicative software.
4. Only propose RRE for capabilities demonstrably missing from incumbent systems (e.g. cross-tool reconciliation, operator-review workflow and traceable attribution). Verify legally authorized integration methods.
5. Measure staff time, outcomes and true incremental profit before quoting an ongoing premium.

## Pricing and buyer validation hypotheses (not quotes)

- **Diagnostic audit**: a tightly scoped, fixed-price optional service, price to be discovered from interviews. Do not use or promise an untested flat fee.
- **Software assistance**: lower-priced self-managed subscription may be needed to compete with built-in CRM workflows; cannot state profitable tier until integrations and support costs are measured.
- **Managed recovery operations**: the previously hypothesized **$700/month** could only be justified if verified integrations, human oversight, ongoing support and *incremental* value significantly exceed the customer's current software. Do not equate this with standalone software pricing.
- **No automatic freemium claim**: giving away managed onboarding may cost more than expected; quantify operator time first.

## Ten-interview falsification checklist

Segment prospects by (a) Jobber/Housecall Pro/ServiceTitan/Hatch users and (b) contractors with disconnected/no CRM. Capture no prospect identity/PII in public GitHub. With permission, ask:

1. What CRM and tier do you use now, and which follow-up automations are already enabled?
2. What specific inquiries or unsold quotes fall through *despite* those tools?
3. Which system owns the authoritative job state and the confirmed customer payment?
4. What permission/opt-out procedure and human approvals are necessary?
5. Would a one-time leakage audit be preferable to a new software subscription?
6. What measurable ongoing service would justify a **$700/month** managed fee? Ask them to reject it if unjustified.
7. What operational workload, integration rights and legal reviews would block a pilot?

**Gates to change strategy:** Require independent buyer evidence (not page views or vendor claims), willingness-to-pay confirmations in written accepted terms and verified costs. If operators already solve the problem cheaply, narrow the wedge or pivot rather than copy.
